Motor / Propulsion Failure

Immediate actions and decision guidance when thrust is partially or fully lost. This page is intended for fast reference under stress.

1. Recognize the Situation

You are likely experiencing a Motor / Propulsion Failure if:
  • Sudden yaw, roll, or tilt without stick input
  • Loss of altitude despite increased throttle
  • Unusual vibration or audible motor change
  • Vehicle spins or drifts uncontrollably

Do not assume sensor or control failure until propulsion loss is ruled out.

2. Immediate Actions (First Seconds)

Reduce aggressive inputs immediately.
  • Center yaw and roll inputs
  • Avoid rapid throttle changes
  • Stabilize attitude as much as possible

Aggressive corrections often worsen asymmetrical thrust conditions.

3. What the Aircraft Can Still Do

Condition Likely Capability
Partial motor thrust loss Limited control possible, increased power draw
Complete motor failure Stable hover not possible
Fixed-wing / VTOL transition Glide or transition may be possible

Multirotor aircraft cannot maintain stable hover after a full motor failure.

4. Make the Decision

Prioritize a controlled descent over recovery attempts.
  • Choose a clear, low-risk landing area
  • Avoid flying over people or property
  • Accept altitude loss if control improves

Attempting to “fight” the aircraft often leads to complete loss of control.

5. Battery & Power Implications

Remaining motors may draw excessive current.
  • Expect rapid battery depletion
  • Voltage sag may trigger additional failsafes
  • Extended flight is not sustainable

If descent is controllable, land immediately.

6. After Landing

Secure the system before inspection.
  • Disarm and disconnect battery
  • Check motor, ESC, and wiring temperature
  • Inspect propellers for damage or detachment

After recovery, review power margins using Battery Operating Envelope to assess stress under asymmetrical thrust.

Motor failures are time-critical. A controlled descent is always preferable to an uncontrolled recovery attempt.
