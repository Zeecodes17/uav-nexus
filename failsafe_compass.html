Compass / Heading Failure

Immediate actions and decision guidance when heading information is incorrect or unreliable. Designed for fast reference under stress.

1. Recognize the Situation

You are likely experiencing a Compass / Heading Failure if:
  • Aircraft yaws unexpectedly while holding position
  • Forward motion does not align with stick direction
  • Map heading or nose direction appears incorrect
  • RTH or navigation causes the aircraft to fly away

A compass failure corrupts direction, not basic stabilization.

2. Immediate Actions (First Seconds)

Stop trusting navigation immediately.
  • Switch to manual attitude control if available
  • Disengage RTH, loiter, or waypoint modes
  • Stabilize altitude and attitude visually

Navigation systems depend on correct heading information.

3. What Becomes Unreliable

Feature Impact
Return to Home (RTH) May fly in the wrong direction
Waypoint Navigation Incorrect path tracking
Position Hold Yaw instability and drift

These behaviors may appear functional but are directionally wrong.

4. Decide: Manual Return or Land

Visual orientation determines the safest option.
  • If home direction is visually clear → return manually
  • If orientation is uncertain → prepare for controlled landing
  • Avoid long-distance navigation attempts

Distance amplifies heading error.

5. Battery & Control Implications

Heading correction increases pilot workload and power draw.
  • Expect reduced efficiency
  • Hover and yaw corrections consume extra energy
  • Battery margin shrinks faster than planned

Treat available flight time as shorter than indicated.

6. If Heading Stabilizes

Reintroduce navigation assistance cautiously.
  • Verify heading consistency visually
  • Confirm map alignment before trusting navigation
  • Avoid immediate automated return at distance

Intermittent compass errors can reappear under load or yaw.

7. After Landing

Investigate the root cause before the next flight.
  • Check for magnetic interference near the compass
  • Inspect wiring routes and power cables
  • Review mounting location and orientation

Heading errors increase pilot workload and power draw. Use Battery Operating Envelope to understand how reduced margins affect control.

Compass failure is dangerous because it feels subtle. When direction cannot be trusted, simplify the task and land early.
