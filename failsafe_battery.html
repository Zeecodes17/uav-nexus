Battery / Low Voltage

Immediate actions and decision guidance when battery voltage drops unexpectedly or reaches critical levels. Designed for fast reference.

1. Recognize the Situation

You are likely experiencing a Battery / Low Voltage condition if:
  • Sudden power reduction or sluggish throttle response
  • Unexpected descent despite throttle input
  • Voltage or battery warnings appear
  • Aircraft initiates automatic landing or RTH

Voltage drop can occur suddenly under load, even if capacity appeared sufficient earlier.

2. Immediate Actions (First Seconds)

Reduce power demand immediately.
  • Stop aggressive maneuvers
  • Reduce climb and forward speed
  • Stabilize attitude before making navigation decisions

High current draw worsens voltage sag and accelerates loss of control.

3. Decide: Return or Land

Distance and remaining margin decide the outcome.
  • If close to home and stable → prepare for direct return
  • If far or unstable → prepare for immediate landing
  • Avoid prolonged hover while deciding

Hesitation is often more dangerous than committing to a controlled landing.

4. What the Aircraft May Do Automatically

Behavior Implication
Return to Home (RTH) Battery may not support full return
Auto Land Descent at current location
Throttle Limiting Reduced control authority

Automatic behavior may change rapidly as voltage continues to drop.

5. Battery Reality Check

Remaining capacity does not equal remaining power capability.
  • Cold batteries sag faster
  • Aged batteries lose effective capacity
  • High load can trigger sudden cutoff

Assume less margin than indicated when operating near voltage limits.

Use the Return Flight Energy Check during planning to avoid this scenario.

6. Commit to the Outcome

Land while control is still available.
  • Select the safest reachable area
  • Maintain gentle descent
  • Prepare for abrupt power loss near touchdown

A controlled landing with damage is preferable to total loss of control.

7. After Landing

Secure and assess the battery.
  • Disconnect battery immediately
  • Check for swelling, heat, or odor
  • Allow battery to cool before handling

Planning ahead helps avoid this situation. Use Return Flight Energy Check to verify reserve margins before flight.

Battery failures escalate quickly. Early power reduction and decisive landing prevent most catastrophic outcomes.
